Privacy notice

Last updated 6 October 2026

OneStorey is run by The Panatag Company. This explains what the app holds about you, who can see it, and what happens when you leave. It is written to be read rather than to be defensible.

What is collected

Your account: an email address and a password. Nothing else is required to sign up.

Your profile: the name you choose to show, a picture if you add one, a short description if you write one, and a five-letter account code so people can find you deliberately.

Where you are, only if you turn on nearby search: an approximate position, stored separately from your profile and rounded before anyone else sees a distance. Your exact coordinates are never shown to another person.

The places you belong to: which ones, what role you hold in each, which room if the owner recorded one, and when you joined or left.

What you write: posts, comments, polls and votes, concerns you raise, messages in a household thread with an owner, direct messages, and enquiries you send about a place.

Rent, where an owner uses it: what was arranged, what was due, when you marked it paid, and when the owner confirmed it.

If you run a place: the rooms, the people you record as living in them with the contact details you enter, tenancies and stays, money in and out, and any receipts you upload.

Pictures you upload, to a size and format limit. Only JPEG, PNG and WebP are accepted, and PDF for receipts.

If the app breaks and you press the button to tell us: the error message you were shown, which screen you were on, and whether you are on a phone or the web. Nothing is sent unless you press it.

What is never collected

No identity documents, no government numbers, no biometrics, and no photographs of a person for verification. The app has no place to put them and never asks.

No payment card details. OneStorey does not take payments and never holds your money — rent moves between you and the owner as it always did, and the app only records that it happened.

No tracking of what you do in other apps, and no advertising identifiers.

Who can see it

The people in the places you belong to can see what you post there, your name and your picture.

The owner of a place you rent in can see your household with them: your rent record, your concerns, and your messages to them. No other renter can see any of it.

An owner cannot see another owner’s books, and a renter cannot see another renter’s rent. This is enforced by the database itself, not by the app, so it holds even against someone using the data connection directly.

A direct message is between the two of you and nobody else.

A post you make anonymously in Community shows no name to other renters. The owner cannot unmask it either.

What the operator can see

Stated plainly because it is true and most apps do not say it. Your records are stored in ordinary readable form on our hosting provider. The person who runs OneStorey has the administrative key to that database and can therefore technically read anything in it, including rent records and private messages.

They do not, and there is no screen in either app that shows them. The key is used for changing the database’s structure, not for reading people’s rows.

If that is not good enough for something, do not put that something in the app. Encrypting records so that even the operator cannot read them is a real intention and is not built yet.

Who else it is shared with

Nobody is sold anything, and no advertising is sold against you.

The app runs on Supabase, which hosts the database and the uploaded files.

Password reset emails are sent through Resend.

If you install from an app store, that store knows you installed it. OneStorey does not send them anything about you.

How long it is kept

For as long as the place exists and you are part of it. A tenancy record is meant to last — it is the thing that lets you show a future landlord that you paid.

If you leave a place, your membership is marked as ended rather than erased, so that the history other people rely on still reads.

Closing your account

You can close it yourself, in Profile, and it takes effect immediately.

Your name, picture and description are removed, your sign-in is destroyed, and you cannot sign in again. Anything you wrote stays where it was and reads as "Deleted account" — otherwise there would be holes in other people’s conversations and a rent ledger that no longer balances.

Places you own by yourself are deleted entirely. If you own a place other people are still in, the app will refuse and tell you which one: those renters’ records are theirs, not yours to erase. Hand the place over or empty it first.

One honest limit: a profile picture you uploaded is no longer shown anywhere, but the file itself is not yet deleted from storage. Removing it needs a server-side job that does not exist yet.

Your rights

Under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 you may ask what is held about you, have it corrected, object to how it is used, and have it erased. Closing your account does the last of those for everything that is only about you.

You can also complain to the National Privacy Commission.

Children

OneStorey is not for people under 18. It is an app about renting property, which is not something a child does, and every account declares its age before it is made.

We do not knowingly collect anything from a child. If we learn that an account belongs to somebody under 18, we close it and remove what it posted. If you are a parent or guardian and believe your child has an account here, write to thepanatagcompany@gmail.com and we will remove it.

We ask for a declaration rather than a date of birth. A birthday is more information than the question needs, and holding one would mean protecting a piece of personal data we have no other use for.
